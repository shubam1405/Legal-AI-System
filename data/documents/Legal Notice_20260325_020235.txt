    c)  That the Parliament of India enacted the Prevention of Corruption (Amendment) Act, 2018, which introduced, inter alia, Section 17A into the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (hereinafter referred to as "the Act"). The said Section 17A came into effect from 26th July, 2018.

    d)  That Section 17A of the Act reads as under:
        "**17A. Enquiry or Inquiry or investigation of offences relatable to recommendations made or decision taken by public servant in discharge of official functions or duties.**- No police officer shall conduct any enquiry or inquiry or investigation into any offence alleged to have been committed by a public servant under this Act, where the alleged offence is relatable to any recommendation made or decision taken by such public servant in discharge of his official functions or duties, without the previous approval—
        (a) in the case of a person who is or was employed, at the time when the offence was alleged to have been committed, in connection with the affairs of the Union, of that Government;
        (b) in the case of a person who is or was employed, at the time when the offence was alleged to have been committed, in connection with the affairs of a State, of that Government;

        Provided that no such approval shall be necessary for cases involving arrest of a person on the spot on the charge of accepting or attempting to accept any undue advantage for himself or for any other person:

2.  **GRIEVANCE/CAUSE OF ACTION:**

    a)  That the introduction of Section 17A of the Act is arbitrary, discriminatory, and violative of Articles 14 and 21 of the Constitution of India, as it creates an unreasonable classification and grants undue protection to public servants, thereby impeding the fair and impartial administration of justice.

    b)  That Section 17A effectively reintroduces a prior approval mechanism for investigation against public servants, which is substantially similar to provisions previously struck down by the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India in landmark judgments, including *Vineet Narain and Others vs. Union of India and Another* (1998) 1 SCC 226 and *Subramanian Swamy vs. Director, Central Bureau of Investigation and Another* (2014) 8 SCC 682. The said judgments unequivocally held that such prior approval requirements for *investigation* are unconstitutional as they act as a shield for corrupt public servants and obstruct the course of justice.

    c)  That the requirement of prior approval for conducting any enquiry, inquiry, or investigation into offences alleged to have been committed by a public servant under the Act, particularly when relatable to recommendations made or decisions taken in discharge of official functions, will inevitably lead to delays, manipulation of evidence, and ultimately, the stifling of genuine anti-corruption efforts.

    d)  That the provision extends this protection to all levels of public servants, making it even more pervasive and obstructive than its predecessors, which were limited to certain categories of public servants. This blanket protection is detrimental to public interest and accountability.

    e)  That the experience with similar prior approval provisions has demonstrated their potential for abuse and their tendency to be used to shield corrupt officials, as evidenced by empirical data on refusal rates for such approvals.

    b)  **Article 21 of the Constitution of India:** Which guarantees the right to life and personal liberty, as effective investigation and prosecution of corruption is an integral part of good governance and ensures a corruption-free society, which is essential for the dignity and well-being of citizens.

    c)  **The ratio decidendi of the Hon'ble Supreme Court judgments in:**
